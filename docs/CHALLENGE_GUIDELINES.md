# 🐍 Python Challenge Guidelines

These guidelines establish how members should approach, complete, submit, discuss, and learn from Python challenges.

## 1. 🎯 The Objective

The purpose of each challenge is to develop independent problem-solving, Python proficiency, logical reasoning, and the ability to explain code.

Getting the correct output matters, but understanding why your solution works is equally important.

## 2. 🧠 The First 30 Minutes

During the first 30 minutes of attempting a challenge, members may use only:

- Their own notes.
- Official Python documentation.
- A debugger.
- `print()` statements for inspecting program behaviour.

During this initial period, do not use AI tools, general internet searches, or ready-made solutions.

The objective is to give yourself a genuine opportunity to reason through the problem independently.

## 3. 🔎 When You Get Stuck

After the initial 30 minutes, you may consult AI tools or other learning resources, unless a challenge specifies additional restrictions.

When using external assistance:

- Understand the information before applying it.
- Develop and test your own implementation.
- Be prepared to explain your code and reasoning.
- Clearly disclose where and how AI assisted you, as required by the challenge.

Using a resource is not automatically a problem. Using code you cannot explain undermines the purpose of the exercise.

Read the [AI Usage Policy](AI_USAGE_POLICY.md) for further guidance.

## 4. 📤 Completion Confirmation and Submission

When you complete a challenge, send the agreed completion confirmation:

**✅ Solved**

This confirms completion; it does not replace any required code submission, explanation, or reflection.

Follow the instructions provided for the particular challenge and use the designated submission workflow.

If you have not completed the challenge, do not claim that you have. You can still participate in the discussion and learn from the experience.

## 5. 🔒 Solution Confidentiality

Before the challenge deadline and designated group discussion:

- Do not post your solution in the group.
- Do not share code, screenshots, or hints that reveal the solution to other members.
- Do not publish your solution in a publicly accessible repository.
- Do not expose another member's private submission.

These rules protect everyone's opportunity to attempt the problem independently.

## 6. 💬 Post-Challenge Discussion and Review

After the deadline, the group will discuss the challenge before reference solutions are released.

Members should be prepared to discuss:

- Their approach to the problem.
- The reasoning behind their implementation.
- Difficulties and unexpected behaviour encountered.
- Errors and how they were resolved.
- Alternative approaches and their trade-offs.
- What they would change if they attempted the problem again.

Screenshots, code explanations, debugging evidence, and reflections may be required for individual challenges.

Reference solutions should be reviewed and released at the appropriate stage, after the designated discussion.

## 7. 🪞 Reflection and Debugging

Reflection is part of the learning process, not a measure of intelligence.

When required by a challenge, document your reflection and debugging experience as instructed.

Consider:

- What did I learn?
- What did I find difficult?
- What errors or unexpected behaviour did I encounter?
- How did I identify and resolve them?
- Where did external assistance help me?
- What would I improve in another attempt?

Be honest about your experience. Struggling with a concept is useful information about what to practise next.

## 8. 🤝 Respect and Integrity

Members should respect different learning speeds, ask constructive questions, and support one another without revealing solutions prematurely.

Mistakes are expected. Be honest about your attempts, assistance, and understanding.

The goal is collective improvement, not competition over who finishes first.

## 9. 📈 Continuous Progress

Challenges will gradually introduce new concepts while reinforcing previously learned material.

Members should revisit weak areas, practise earlier concepts, and understand the connection between topics rather than rushing through the roadmap.

**Boss battles** will draw on previously covered material as well as more recent topics. They are intended to test accumulated understanding, not just short-term recall.

## 10. ✅ The Standard

**Attempt independently. Understand deeply. Explain clearly. Learn from mistakes. Improve continuously.**
